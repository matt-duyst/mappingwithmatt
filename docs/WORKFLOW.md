# How a change moves through the checks

Work happens on a branch, never directly on main, and each step of a brief becomes one commit whose message says what changed in the site rather than what was done to the repository: "Reserve the height of every image before it loads", not "fix CSS". A commit is pushed only after it has been verified on a local server, and the next step is not started until the previous one is on origin. Reviews are served with `python3 tools/serve.py`, which serves the site on 0.0.0.0:8000 with `Cache-Control: no-store` on every response, so a reload never shows a stale file.

Before a change is merged it is looked at, not only measured. Captures are taken at 1440 and 390 pixels wide, in the dark theme and the light theme, of whatever the change touches, and they are judged by eye against what the brief asked for. The captures are kept in a dated review folder beside the repository, not inside it.

Every sentence on the seven content pages that contains a number, a year, a percentage, a unit, a named institution or a title has a row in tools/claims/ledger.tsv naming the page, the sentence, the source and the line or heading that states it. The sources are the project READMEs, the CV, the figure files and pages the site already links to. When a sentence with such content is written or rewritten, its ledger row is written or rewritten in the same commit, before the push; a sentence that cannot be sourced keeps a row marked UNSOURCED and is listed with its open question in tools/claims/UNSOURCED.md, and it is not changed to fit a source it does not have. The workflow in .github/workflows/check.yml runs tools/claims/check.py on every push and pull request to main, together with the internal-link sweep and axe on all seven pages at both widths in both themes, and it refuses the push when a sentence with a digit has no ledger row, when a link is broken, or when a page has an accessibility violation, a console error or horizontal overflow.

Performance is measured with tools/audit/run.py in headless Chrome rendering with the GPU, never with the software renderer, whose first frame alone costs more than the whole sequence being measured. Two profiles are recorded for every page: the throttled one, 40 ms of round-trip time, 10 Mbps and a device pixel ratio of 1, is the quoted figure, taken as the median of three runs with all three kept beside it; the local one, unthrottled at the device pixel ratio of a real screen, is recorded beside it and labelled local. Continuous integration also measures performance, on a GPU-less runner against its own CI baseline, as the section below describes; the local GPU baseline remains the reference for local runs.

tools/audit/baseline.json is the state the next audit is compared with. It is refreshed with tools/audit/compare.py --write-baseline, which refuses when any gate fails against the previous baseline: requests or bytes up more than ten percent, first or largest contentful paint up more than a hundred milliseconds, cumulative layout shift above 0.05, total blocking time above 50 ms, any accessibility violation, any broken link. A baseline can be forced with --force-baseline and a reason, and the file then records that reason and the metrics that failed, so a later reader knows the line was moved on purpose and why.

## Measuring in CI

A branch under review is measured by .github/workflows/review.yml on a GitHub-hosted ubuntu-latest runner, which has no GPU, so Chrome renders in software there and its figures are compared only with tools/audit/baseline-ci.json, a baseline taken on the same kind of runner, never with the local GPU baseline.

Timing is gated by a paired comparison against main on one runner. On every push of a branch other than main, the workflow's timing job checks out main's head and the branch's head, serves each on its own port, and loads them alternately with tools/audit/paired.py: nine rounds in each of four cells, 1440 and 390 wide in the dark and light themes, the order flipping every round, each load cold and then scrolled through the page. For cold total blocking time, scroll total blocking time, first contentful paint and largest contentful paint, it takes the nine round-by-round differences, branch minus main. A cell's metric fails only when at least eight of the nine differences show the branch slower, a one-sided sign test with a chance of about 2 percent, and the median difference exceeds the threshold: 50 milliseconds for cold blocking time, 10 percent of main's median for scroll blocking time, and 100 milliseconds for either paint. Missing or empty data is an error, never a pass. The decision is the function paired_failure in tools/audit/trend.py. Runners differ from each other by more than most changes do, and a stored baseline was recorded on a different runner, so timing is compared only between two pages loaded on the same one.

Sizes, requests and layout shift stay on the stored baseline, since they do not vary with runner speed. The audit loads the branch cold nine times in each of the four cells and fails when cold requests or bytes rise more than ten percent over tools/audit/baseline-ci.json, or when layout shift exceeds 0.05. Its comparison of blocking time and paint timing with the stored baseline stays in the job summary as a report-only trend line, the median against the baseline's minimum-to-maximum range.

A branch may carry an allowance the owner has approved, passed to compare.py and printed in the job summary with its reason. Section three carries two. Cold bytes may rise 15 percent over the baseline instead of 10, for the record and the reading type. Cold requests may exceed the ten percent gate by one, for the self-hosted Fraunces italic of the About's opening line. Each allowance belongs to its branch and ends when that branch merges.

Any change made for performance is timed as a pair before it is kept: tools/audit/paired.py loads the commit before the change and the commit with it on one runner, nine cold runs each at 1440 in both themes, alternating which goes first every round, and the change is kept only when its median total blocking time falls by more than the larger of the two sides' spreads.

A change that could alter how the hero looks is checked at a frozen clock: the page accepts ?clock=<ms> for review, which fixes the blob's time and seeds its layout, and tools/audit/herolook.py captures the hero on both commits at 0, 5 and 20 seconds, at 1440 in both themes and at 390 in dark, and saves each pair side by side for the owner with the share of differing pixels and the largest channel difference. Without the parameter the page behaves as it always does, which the same tool confirms by comparing the DOM and the console of a normal load on both commits.

After every merge into main the CI baseline is taken again from the new main, nine runs, through the review workflow's baseline mode, and committed with its reason, so the next branch is measured against the site as it now stands.

Nothing in the repository, its commits or its documents attributes work to any tool. What is recorded is what changed, what was measured and what was decided.
