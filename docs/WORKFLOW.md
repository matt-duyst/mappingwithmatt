# How a change moves through the checks

Work happens on a branch, never directly on main, and each step of a brief becomes one commit whose message says what changed in the site rather than what was done to the repository: "Reserve the height of every image before it loads", not "fix CSS". A commit is pushed only after it has been verified on a local server, and the next step is not started until the previous one is on origin.

Before a change is merged it is looked at, not only measured. Captures are taken at 1440 and 390 pixels wide, in the dark theme and the light theme, of whatever the change touches, and they are judged by eye against what the brief asked for. The captures are kept in a dated review folder beside the repository, not inside it.

Every sentence on the seven content pages that contains a number, a year, a percentage, a unit, a named institution or a title has a row in tools/claims/ledger.tsv naming the page, the sentence, the source and the line or heading that states it. The sources are the project READMEs, the CV, the figure files and pages the site already links to. When a sentence with such content is written or rewritten, its ledger row is written or rewritten in the same commit, before the push; a sentence that cannot be sourced keeps a row marked UNSOURCED and is listed with its open question in tools/claims/UNSOURCED.md, and it is not changed to fit a source it does not have. The workflow in .github/workflows/check.yml runs tools/claims/check.py on every push and pull request to main, together with the internal-link sweep and axe on all seven pages at both widths in both themes, and it refuses the push when a sentence with a digit has no ledger row, when a link is broken, or when a page has an accessibility violation, a console error or horizontal overflow.

Performance is measured with tools/audit/run.py in headless Chrome rendering with the GPU, never with the software renderer, whose first frame alone costs more than the whole sequence being measured. Two profiles are recorded for every page: the throttled one, 40 ms of round-trip time, 10 Mbps and a device pixel ratio of 1, is the quoted figure, taken as the median of three runs with all three kept beside it; the local one, unthrottled at the device pixel ratio of a real screen, is recorded beside it and labelled local. Continuous integration never measures performance; it only checks.

tools/audit/baseline.json is the state the next audit is compared with. It is refreshed with tools/audit/compare.py --write-baseline, which refuses when any gate fails against the previous baseline: requests or bytes up more than ten percent, first or largest contentful paint up more than a hundred milliseconds, cumulative layout shift above 0.05, total blocking time above 50 ms, any accessibility violation, any broken link. A baseline can be forced with --force-baseline and a reason, and the file then records that reason and the metrics that failed, so a later reader knows the line was moved on purpose and why.

Nothing in the repository, its commits or its documents attributes work to any tool. What is recorded is what changed, what was measured and what was decided.
